Who I Am

Curious about how it all fits together

I'm Siphiwe Khumalo — most people call me Ronnie. I'm interested in technology as a whole system, not just the part that's visible on screen. A slow computer, a flaky connection, a site that won't load, a business process held together with spreadsheets — I want to know what's actually happening underneath, and then fix it.

Day to day that means I move between different layers of the same problem: sorting out a hardware or network issue one moment, writing and shipping code the next. I currently split my time between freelance development work and hands-on IT support, alongside studies in Tourism — a background that keeps me thinking about technology from the perspective of the people actually using it, not just the systems themselves.

How I Work

One system, six layers

Most problems don't stay in one layer. A "slow website" might actually be a network issue. A "broken system" might just be a hardware fault upstream. I try to start wherever the problem is actually happening, not wherever it's easiest for me to look.

This is roughly how I think about the stack — and where in it I tend to spend my time.

01

Hardware

Diagnosing and fixing the physical machines — PCs, peripherals, the stuff that fails first.

02

Networks

Getting devices talking to each other reliably, from Wi-Fi to wired infrastructure.

03

Infrastructure

The wiring behind the scenes — including systems like CCTV and VoIP that businesses depend on daily.

04

Cloud & Systems

Microsoft 365, cloud services, and the administration that keeps them running smoothly.

05

Software & Web

Building the applications and websites people actually interact with.

06

Users & Businesses

Where all of the above has to translate into something that just works for the person relying on it.

Where I'm Heading

Staying broad, on purpose

I could narrow down to just IT support, or just development. I'm not in a hurry to do either. The version of my career I'm most interested in is one where the hardware, network, infrastructure, and software knowledge all keep informing each other — because in practice, that's how most real problems actually show up.

Longer term, that probably means growing into a role that sits across IT and technical systems more broadly, while keeping software and web development as a skill I keep sharp rather than one I set aside.
